1 Nothing to show

Delete everything and your app shows a blank page. A real app tells the user what is going on.

🎯 The goal
  • When the list has no items, the page shows Your list is empty in the middle, instead of a blank space.
  • As soon as an item is added, the list comes back.
Delete Milk, Bread and Rice: Your list is empty appears. Add Eggs: the list is back.
💡 Hint 1

What goes inside the Expanded now depends on the data: a message when items is empty, the ListView otherwise.

💡 Hint 2

You used condition ? a : b in step 8 for the crossed-out text. It works for whole widgets too: items.isEmpty ? Center(...) : ListView.builder(...).

2 How many?

Nobody buys "milk". People buy 3 bottles of milk.

🎯 The goal
  • Every item has a quantity, starting at 1, shown on its row between a − and a + button, before the bin.
  • + adds one. − removes one, but the quantity never goes below 1. To remove an item completely, there is the bin.
Tap + on Milk twice: 3. Tap − four times: it stops at 1.
💡 Hint 1

The quantity belongs to the item, so it goes in the model: a new field in Item, starting at 1. Then do a hot restart. You know why from step 8.

💡 Hint 2

The row's trailing takes one widget, and you need four (−, the number, +, the bin). Put them in a Row. The icons are Icons.remove and Icons.add.

💡 Hint 3

"Never below 1" is a rule, like in your labs: check before you change.

🆘 Classic trap

Trailing widget consumes the entire tile width: a Row takes all the width it can get, which leaves nothing for the name. Tell it to be only as wide as its children: Row(mainAxisSize: MainAxisSize.min, children: [...]).

3 No duplicates, just more

Typing "milk" when Milk is already there should not give you two rows of milk.

🎯 The goal
  • Adding an item that is already on the list, whatever its capitals or spaces (milk,  MILK ), does not add a new row.
  • Instead, that item's quantity goes up by 1, and a message says Milk is already on the list: now ×2.
  • A new item is still added as before.
Type  mILK and tap Add: still three rows, Milk shows 2, and the message appears.
💡 Hint 1

Compare both names in the same case. Lab 4 had the tool for that.

💡 Hint 2

items.where((item) => ...) gives you the items that match. Then .isNotEmpty tells you if there is one, and .first gives it to you.

4 Oops: undo

Every good app forgives mistakes. Delete the wrong item, and you get it back.

🧠 New Flutter piece: a button inside the message

A SnackBar can carry one button, called an action:

SnackBar(
  content: Text('Bread removed'),
  action: SnackBarAction(
    label: 'Undo',
    onPressed: () {
      // what happens when Undo is tapped
    },
  ),
)

Before showing a new message, ScaffoldMessenger.of(context).hideCurrentSnackBar(); removes the old one, so messages do not queue up.

🎯 The goal
  • Deleting an item shows Bread removed with an Undo button.
  • Undo puts the item back in exactly the same place, with its quantity and bought status.
Delete Bread, tap Undo: the list is Milk, Bread, Rice again, not Milk, Rice, Bread.
💡 Hint 1

To put it back in the same place, you must know where it was. Find that out before you remove it.

💡 Hint 2

items.indexOf(item) gives an item's position, and items.insert(position, item) puts it back there. Lab 10 used insert.

5 Show me what's left

In the shop, you only want to see what you still need to buy.

🧠 New Flutter piece: choice chips

A ChoiceChip is a small button that can be selected. Keep the chosen filter in your State, as String filter = 'All';

ChoiceChip(
  label: const Text('All'),
  selected: filter == 'All',
  onSelected: (_) {
    setState(() {
      filter = 'All';
    });
  },
)
🎯 The goal
  • Three chips under the input row: All, To buy and Bought. The selected one is highlighted.
  • All shows every item, with the bought ones always at the bottom. To buy shows only items not bought. Bought shows only bought items.
  • When a filter has nothing to show, the page says Nothing here. (Part 1's message stays for a truly empty list.)
  • Everything still works in every filter: marking, −, +, delete and undo.
Tap Milk to mark it: in All it moves to the bottom. Bought shows only Milk. Delete it there: Nothing here. Undo: Milk is back. All: Bread, Rice, Milk.
💡 Hint 1

Do not change items to filter it. In build, make a second list, shown, from items, and give shown to the ListView.

💡 Hint 2

items.where(...).toList() makes a filtered list. Two lists join into one with [...first, ...second]: the ... spreads a list's items into the new one.

💡 Hint 3

Look at your delete. index is now a position in shown, not in items. Delete the item itself: items.remove(item).

🆘 Classic trap

The wrong item gets deleted, and there is no error message at all: you still remove by index, but with a filter on, row 0 of the screen is not item 0 of your list. The most dangerous bugs are the silent ones. Test every action in every filter.

6 Item details

Every real app has a second screen. Tap into an item and see everything about it.

🧠 New Flutter piece: a second screen

A new screen is just another widget class. Navigator.push opens it on top, and Navigator.pop(context) goes back. You pass it the item through its constructor, with a named parameter, like HomeScreen(title: ...):

Navigator.push(
  context,
  MaterialPageRoute(builder: (context) => DetailsScreen(item: item)),
).then((_) {
  setState(() {});
});

.then(...) runs when the user comes back. The details screen changed the item, so the list must redraw.

The details screen needs its fields filled with the item's current values when it opens. initState runs once, when a screen first appears:

@override
void initState() {
  super.initState();
  nameController.text = widget.item.name;
}
🎯 The goal
  • A long-press on an item opens a new screen, Item details, with a back arrow at the top.
  • It shows the item's name and a note (for example the 1 litre bottle) in two text fields, and its category as three chips: Food, Drinks, Home. New items start as Food with no note.
  • Save keeps the changes and goes back to the list. An empty name is refused with a message, and you stay on the screen.
  • The back arrow goes back without changing anything.
  • On the list, each row shows its category under the name, and the note after it: Drinks · the 1 litre bottle.
Long-press Rice, rename it Brown rice, note 1 kg bag, category Home, Save: the row says Brown rice, with Home · 1 kg bag under it. Long-press Milk, change the name, then use the back arrow: still Milk.
💡 Hint 1

The item needs two new fields in your model: a note and a category. Hot restart afterwards.

💡 Hint 2

Make DetailsScreen a StatefulWidget with required this.item, like HomeScreen has required this.title. Its State has two controllers and the chosen category. The chips work exactly like your filter chips from part 5.

💡 Hint 3

Write the changes into widget.item only when Save is tapped. That is why the back arrow changes nothing.

🆘 Classic trap

'name' can't be used as a setter because it's final: final means "set once, never changed", and now the name must change. Remove final from name in your Item class.

🆘 Classic trap

You save, go back, and the list still shows the old name, with no error: the item did change, but nobody told the list to redraw. That is what the .then((_) { setState(() {}); }) after Navigator.push is for.

⚡ Push further
  • Give each item a price (a second field when adding) and show the total still to spend at the bottom: price × quantity, only for items not bought. Refuse a price that is not a number.
  • A button that sorts the list A to Z.
